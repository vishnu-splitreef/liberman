Our Zoning and Land Development Legal Services

Land development is highly local and requires familiarity with specific regional regulations. Jacksonville operates as a consolidated city-county government, which creates a unique regulatory landscape. Our practice area covers every aspect of the approval process that developers, landowners, investors, and businesses encounter in Duval County.

Rezonings and Planned Unit Developments (PUDs)

We handle conventional rezonings and PUD applications and rezonings, prepare the required written descriptions and site plans, and present your case through the Planning Commission, LUZ Committee, and City Council hearings.

Variances, special exceptions, and waivers

When a lot’s shape, size, or existing conditions don’t fit neatly within Chapter 656’s dimensional standards, we pursue variances, special exceptions, road frontage waivers, and sign waivers, and we handle administrative deviations under Section 656.109 for smaller-scale relief.

Development permits and plat approval

Legal services include obtaining permits and approvals for development – from preliminary and final plat review to concurrency determinations and capacity letters. We manage the subdivision platting process governed by Chapter 654, handle site development plan submissions, and track your project through the city’s nine-step review procedure outlined in the Land Development Procedure Manual. For property developers and investors, our goal is faster project approvals with fewer revision cycles.

Regulatory Compliance and Government Relations

Navigating Jacksonville city and county requirements demands more than filing paperwork. Land development processes involve negotiations with local agencies and regulatory bodies. We assist with compliance regarding environmentally sensitive lands, coastal development issues, wetlands and water-management permitting, stormwater management requirements, and federal environmental regulations. We also help developers secure economic incentives through legal counsel – including mobility fee reductions, stormwater quality credits, and historic preservation incentives available through the Jacksonville Downtown Investment Authority.

Land Use Litigation

When development applications are denied or when government actions threaten your property rights, we provide aggressive representation. Attorneys represent clients in land use litigation and administrative proceedings, including zoning appeals, challenges under Florida’s Bert Harris Act for inordinate burden claims, comprehensive plan consistency disputes, and legal representation against governmental actions. Dispute resolution services are essential for managing zoning appeals and administrative decisions, and our litigation team has experience in both state court and before local administrative bodies.

How Our Land Development Process Works

We streamline Jacksonville’s complex development approval process so you can focus on building, not bureaucracy.

Step 1: Initial Site Assessment and Strategy

During your free consultation, our attorneys analyze your property’s development potential and regulatory requirements. We review current zoning classifications, identify applicable land use restrictions, evaluate environmental constraints including wetlands and flood zones, and estimate the timeline and costs for your specific project. Understanding impact fees is vital for managing infrastructure obligations in development – for example, a new single-family home in Jacksonville carries at least $6,284 per unit in impact fees as of July 2026, with major line items being sewer capacity ($5,114) and water capacity ($1,170). We make sure you know these numbers before you commit.

Step 2: Permit Applications and Government Approvals

We prepare all application materials, present your project at planning board hearings, and manage the back-and-forth with city reviewers. Developers must be aware of zoning codes and local staff expectations – our firm handles that coordination. For subdivision projects, the entire process from preliminary plat through final recording generally totals about 115 working days under standard procedures, with key phases including pre-application conferences (15–45 days), preliminary site plan review (~28 days), and final site plan review (~14 days). Large-scale projects often require negotiation of impact fees and development agreements, and we handle those discussions on your behalf.

Development Projects We’ve Supported

Each city or county has its own zoning ordinances, and our focus on Jacksonville means we bring specific, actionable knowledge to every engagement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q.01How much do land development legal services cost in Jacksonville?

Attorney fees are typically structured as hourly rates or project-based fees depending on complexity. Separately, the City of Jacksonville publishes specific application fees: conventional rezoning runs approximately $2,000 plus $10 per acre, PUD applications cost around $2,269 plus per-acre charges, and preliminary plat review is $700 plus $27 per lot. These city fees are in addition to legal fees. During your free consultation, we provide a clear estimate based on your project’s scope so there are no surprises.

Q.02Do I need City Council approval for my project?

Not always. Administrative deviations and many exceptions and variances are handled without a full City Council vote. Conventional and PUD rezonings, along with road frontage and sign waivers, do require City Council action after Planning Commission and LUZ Committee review. We’ll tell you which path applies once we know your zoning district and what you’re proposing.

Q.03How long does the Jacksonville development approval process typically take?

Timelines vary by project type. For subdivision and plat approvals, the standard process runs approximately 115 working days from preliminary submission through final plat recording. Rezoning applications and public hearings add additional weeks or months depending on scheduling and whether there is community opposition. Some simpler residential plan reviews may be completed in as few as seven days for certain review steps. We set realistic timeline expectations for your specific project during our initial assessment.

Q.04What happens if our development application gets denied?

A denial is not the end of the road. Options include filing formal appeals, seeking variances or exceptions, pursuing judicial review of comprehensive plan consistency, or filing claims under Florida’s Bert Harris Act if government action creates an inordinate burden on your property. In City of Jacksonville v. Smith (2015), for example, property owners pursued Bert Harris Act claims seeking compensation for diminished property value after adjacent rezoning – the kind of complex litigation our firm is prepared to handle. We evaluate every avenue available to protect your development rights.

Q.05Do you handle both residential and commercial development projects?

Yes. Our practice encompasses single-family home permits, residential subdivisions, multifamily projects, commercial office and retail development, industrial sites, and mixed-use projects. Zoning laws can affect residential and commercial property development very differently – density requirements, intensity standards, environmental compliance, and concurrency obligations all shift depending on project type. We also support land acquisition, contract contingencies, and financing matters connected to your development. Comprehensive legal services encompass regulatory, transactional, and litigation support, and that is exactly what we provide.
