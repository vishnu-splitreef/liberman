Can a Quit Claim Deed be Contested in FL?

The Short Answer

Yes. A quit claim deed can be contested in Florida, but only for a legal defect, not because someone regrets the transfer. The strongest grounds are forgery, fraud, undue influence, lack of mental capacity, a deed never actually delivered, a homestead transfer signed without the spouse, and missing witnesses.

Challenges are brought in circuit court, usually as a quiet title or cancellation-of-deed lawsuit. A forged deed is void and can be attacked at any time; most other claims face deadlines of four to seven years, and waiting can cost you the case.

Most quit claim deeds in Florida are signed at a kitchen table, not a closing table. A parent adds a child to the title. A spouse signs away an interest during a divorce. A sibling “cleans up” the title after a funeral. The document is short, the form is easy to find online, and nobody involved expects a fight.

The fights come later, often after the person who signed has died or lost capacity, when relatives discover a deed they never knew about. This guide explains what actually makes a Florida quit claim deed invalid, which lawsuits are used to undo one, how the deadlines work, and what changes when the grantor has passed away.

01

What a Quit Claim Deed Does, and What It Doesn’t

A quit claim deed transfers whatever interest the grantor has in the property at the moment of signing, and nothing more. If the grantor owns the whole property, the grantee gets the whole property. If the grantor owns half, or nothing, that is what passes. The grantor makes no promise that the title is good or free of liens.

That is the key difference from a warranty deed. A Florida warranty deed carries covenants that the grantor owns the property, has the right to convey it, and will defend the title against lawful claims. If a title problem surfaces, the grantee can sue the grantor on those promises. With a quit claim deed, there is nothing to sue on.

One common misunderstanding is worth clearing up: a quit claim deed is not weaker at transferring ownership. If the grantor owns the property free and clear, a properly signed quit claim deed conveys it just as completely as a warranty deed. The weakness is in the protection it gives the grantee, not in the transfer itself.

02

Florida Quit Claim Deed Laws: What Makes a Deed Valid

Before looking at how to attack a deed, it helps to know what a valid one requires. Florida’s rules come from a few different statutes, and they serve different purposes:

Two subscribing witnesses

Under section 689.01, Florida Statutes, a deed conveying real property must be signed by the grantor in the presence of two witnesses who also sign. This is a validity requirement, not just a recording formality.

Acknowledgment before a notary

A deed must be acknowledged under section 695.03 to be recorded. The notary confirms the signer’s identity; the notary is not one of the two witnesses unless they also sign as a witness.

Recording formalities
Section 695.26 requires printed names beneath signatures, the grantee’s address, and the preparer’s name and address. Since January 1, 2024, it also requires each witness’s post office address, and clerks such as Duval County’s reject deeds without it.
A legal description that identifies the land

A street address alone is usually not enough. The deed should carry the full legal description from the prior vesting deed.

Delivery and acceptance

The grantor must intend to transfer ownership now and hand over the deed (or record it) with that intent. A signed deed left in a drawer, meant to take effect only at death, may never have been delivered at all.

Recording is where people often get confused. A deed does not have to be recorded to be valid between the grantor and grantee. Recording under section 695.01 protects the grantee against later buyers and creditors who had no notice of the transfer. So an unrecorded deed can still be real, and a recorded deed can still be void.

03

What Voids a Quit Claim Deed in Florida?

Courts draw a sharp line between deeds that are void (legally nothing from the start) and deeds that are voidable (valid until a court sets them aside). The difference controls deadlines, what happens to later buyers, and how hard the case is to win.

Forgery or material alteration

A forged deed is void. It passes no title, even to a later buyer who paid fair value and had no idea. The same goes for a deed altered after signing in a way that changes its substance, such as swapping the grantee’s name. Expect a handwriting expert, the notary’s journal, and the witnesses’ testimony to become central evidence.

Fraud in the inducement

Fraud is different from forgery. Here the grantor really did sign, but only because someone lied about what the document was or what would happen. The Florida Supreme Court in Reed v. Fain treated a deed obtained by fraud as voidable rather than void, which means the claim must be brought in time and can be affected by the rights of innocent later purchasers.

Undue influence

This is the most common ground in family deed disputes, and the reason many quit claim deed undue influence cases in Florida are winnable despite little direct evidence. Florida courts apply a presumption of undue influence, drawn from In re Estate of Carpenter, 253 So. 2d 697 (Fla. 1971), when the person who benefited from the deed had a confidential relationship with the grantor and was active in procuring it. As The Florida Bar Journal explains, active procurement can be shown through facts such as the beneficiary choosing the lawyer, arranging the signing, being present when it happened, and keeping the deed afterward. Once the presumption arises, the grantee has to come forward with a reasonable explanation.

Lack of mental capacity

The grantor must understand, at the moment of signing, that they are giving away property, what property it is, and who is receiving it. Dementia, heavy medication, or a serious illness does not automatically void a deed, because people have lucid periods. Medical records from the days around the signing usually decide these cases. A deed signed by someone already adjudicated incapacitated, with those rights removed, stands on much weaker ground.

Homestead signed without the spouse

Florida’s homestead protections in Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution restrict how a married owner can transfer the family home. A married owner generally cannot convey homestead property unless the spouse joins in the deed. A quit claim deed of homestead signed by one spouse alone can be invalid, which matters in second marriages where a parent deeds the home to children without the current spouse’s signature.

Missing witnesses or no delivery

A deed without two witnesses fails section 689.01. There is an important catch, though: under section 95.231, once a deed has been of record for five years, defects such as missing witnesses or a flawed acknowledgment are generally cured, absent fraud, adverse possession, or pending litigation. Delivery problems are different, and they often come up in after-death disputes, discussed below.

04

How to Contest a Quit Claim Deed in Florida

Challenging a quit claim deed in Florida means filing suit in the circuit court for the county where the property sits. The legal vehicle depends on what you are trying to fix.

  • Quiet title action. Under Chapter 65, Florida Statutes, a quiet title action asks the court to declare who owns the property and remove the cloud created by an invalid deed. It is the most common tool, and our Jacksonville quiet title attorney page covers how these cases typically run in Duval and surrounding counties.
  • Cancellation or rescission of the deed. An equitable claim asking the court to cancel the deed outright, typically paired with fraud, undue influence, or incapacity allegations.
  • Used when the deed is legitimate but contains a mistake, such as a wrong lot number or a misspelled name, and the parties’ true intent can be proven. Reformation fixes the deed rather than undoing it.
  • Under Chapter 64, co-owners who cannot agree can ask the court to divide the property or order it sold. Partition does not attack the deed; it deals with the co-ownership the deed created.

Two practical steps matter early. First, record a notice of lis pendens under section 48.23 when the suit is filed, so no one can sell or mortgage the property out from under the case. Second, pull a full title search before filing. Knowing every recorded instrument, mortgage, and lien on the property shapes who must be named as a defendant, and a title search attorney can often spot problems a quick online records search misses.

05

How Long Do You Have to Contest a Quit Claim Deed in Florida?

There is no single deadline. The time limit depends on the legal theory, and some commonly repeated figures online are wrong. Here is how the main rules actually work:

Forgery

A forged deed is void, and Florida courts have held it can be challenged at any time. Even the 20-year rule in section 95.231 does not protect a forged deed.

Fraud

Generally four years under section 95.11, running from when the fraud was or should have been discovered under section 95.031, but no later than 12 years after the fraud occurred.

Undue influence and lack of capacity

These claims are usually brought as equitable actions to cancel the deed and are commonly measured against the four-year period for comparable legal claims, with laches as an additional defense. Delay hurts these cases badly because witnesses and medical memories fade.

Missing witnesses or acknowledgment defects

Generally cured five years after recording under section 95.231(1), absent fraud, adverse possession, or pending litigation.

Any claim against a recorded deed

After 20 years of record, section 95.231(2) generally bars claims against those holding under the deed, with the forgery exception noted above.

Adverse possession

Seven years of open, continuous possession under section 95.16 and related sections can create title in someone else. This is not a deadline to contest a deed; it is a separate way the other side may claim ownership if you wait.

Because the right deadline depends on how the claim is framed, and courts sometimes disagree on which period applies to equitable claims, assume the shortest plausible period and move quickly.

06

A Bedside Signature and a Deed Found After the Funeral

A matter handled by Lieberman Chamberlain PLLC shows how these rules play out in real families. A widowed Arlington homeowner had long told her three children the house would be split equally. In her final months, one son moved in as her caregiver. After she died, the other two siblings learned a quit claim deed had been recorded two weeks after the funeral, transferring the home solely to the caregiver son.

The deed was dated about five weeks before her death, while she was hospitalized. The son had arranged the notary visit and brought two of his friends as witnesses, and he had kept the original deed at the house. Those facts lined up closely with the active-procurement factors Florida courts look for under the undue influence presumption. Hospital records also showed she was on medication that affected her orientation during that period.

Andy Lieberman filed a quiet title and cancellation action in Duval County on behalf of the two siblings and recorded a lis pendens the same day, which stopped a pending sale the son had already listed. Faced with the presumption and the medical records, the son agreed to a stipulated judgment setting the deed aside, and the house passed through the mother’s estate in equal shares as she had said she wanted.

07

Can a Quit Claim Deed Be Contested After Death in Florida?

Yes, and this is when most challenges happen. The personal representative of the estate, and often heirs or beneficiaries who would have received the property, can bring the claim. A few issues come up again and again:

Recording after death is not automatically fatal

What matters is delivery. If the grantor signed and handed over the deed during life, intending an immediate transfer, recording it after death does not undo it. If the deed sat in the grantor’s own papers and was meant to take effect only at death, it may never have been delivered and may fail.

The property may not be in the probate estate

A valid deed takes the home out of the estate, so the will does not control it. That is why heirs who expected a share under the will often end up in a deed challenge instead of a will contest.

The undue influence presumption is built for these cases

The person who can best explain what happened is gone, so the presumption shifts the burden of explanation to the grantee when the caregiver or relative arranged the deed.

08

Can a Warranty Deed Be Contested?

Yes. Forgery, fraud, undue influence, incapacity, and homestead defects can undo any Florida deed, whether it is a warranty deed, special warranty deed, or quit claim deed. The difference is what happens afterward. A grantee under a warranty deed who loses title to a third party’s superior claim may have a claim against the grantor on the warranty and, usually, coverage under an owner’s title insurance policy. A quit claim grantee usually has neither, which is why buyers in arm’s-length sales almost never accept one.

09

Protecting Yourself Before a Dispute Starts

If you are signing or accepting a quit claim deed, a few precautions prevent most of the disputes described above. Use independent witnesses who are not beneficiaries. If the grantor is elderly or ill, have a lawyer meet with them privately, and consider a note from their physician close to the signing date. Confirm homestead status and get the spouse’s joinder where required. Record promptly. And if you suspect someone may try to transfer your property without your knowledge, many Florida clerks now offer free property fraud alert services that notify owners when a document is recorded in their name.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q.01Can a quitclaim deed be contested if the grantor changed their mind?

Not on that basis alone. Once a valid deed is signed, witnessed, and delivered, the grantor cannot revoke it. The grantee would have to sign a new deed conveying the property back.

Q.02Can a deed be contested if I was never told about it?

Lack of notice is not itself a ground to void a deed, but it often goes hand in hand with fraud or undue influence, and it can affect when the fraud limitations period starts to run.

Q.03What happens if a quit claim deed is set aside?

The court cancels the deed and title returns to where it stood before the transfer. If the grantor has died, the property typically passes through the estate or to whoever held title under the prior deed. The court can also address mortgages or liens the grantee placed on the property, depending on the facts.

Q.04Is a quit claim deed without a notary valid in Florida?

Between the parties, the notary is not what makes a deed valid; the two witnesses are. But the clerk will not record an unacknowledged deed, and an unrecorded deed leaves the grantee exposed to later purchasers and creditors.

Q.05How much does it cost to contest a deed transfer?

It depends heavily on whether the other side fights. Some cases resolve with a corrective or reconveyance deed after a demand letter; others go through discovery, expert witnesses, and trial. Ask any attorney you consult for a realistic estimate based on the evidence available.
