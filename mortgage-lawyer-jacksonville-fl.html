What is a Mortgage Attorney?

A mortgage attorney is a licensed lawyer who specializes in the complex state and federal laws governing real estate financing. Their role is fundamentally different from that of a real estate agent (who finds and negotiates the sale) or a loan officer (who works for the lender to sell you a financial product).

A mortgage lawyer near Jacksonville works for you and only you. Their primary duty is to protect your legal and financial interests.

This role is typically divided into two key practice areas:

Transactional Counsel (The “Before”)

An attorney provides proactive advice to homebuyers before and at the closing. They review the loan estimate, the closing disclosure, the promissory note, and the mortgage deed itself. They explain your obligations, identify red-flag clauses (like balloon payments or steep prepayment penalties), and ensure the terms you agreed to are the terms you are signing.

Litigation & Dispute Resolution (The “After”)

This is where a mortgage attorney becomes a critical advocate. This role involves representing homeowners in disputes against lenders, loan servicers, and other parties. This includes foreclosure defense, filing lawsuits for mortgage fraud, and resolving complex servicing errors.

Lieberman Chamberlain excels in both areas. Our litigation experience makes us sharper transactional attorneys because we know exactly which clauses and practices lead to lawsuits.

Understanding and Fighting Mortgage Fraud

Mortgage fraud is a serious crime that can have devastating consequences for homeowners. It involves intentional misrepresentation or omission of information to secure a mortgage loan that would not have been approved otherwise. Fraud can be perpetrated by lenders, brokers, or even buyers. As a leading Jacksonville mortgage fraud attorney, we are committed to holding responsible parties accountable and protecting victims.

04

Common Types of Mortgage Fraud

Mortgage fraud schemes can take many forms. Being aware of them is the first step toward protection. Some common types include:

  • Predatory Lending: This occurs when lenders impose unfair, deceptive, or abusive loan terms on borrowers. Examples include excessively high interest rates, balloon payments designed to fail, and exorbitant fees.
  • Loan Modification Scams: Fraudsters may pose as legitimate counselors and charge hefty upfront fees for promising to help homeowners modify their loans to avoid foreclosure. Often, they do little to no work and disappear with the money.
  • Foreclosure Rescue Scams: These schemes target homeowners in distress, offering to “rescue” them from foreclosure. The fraudster might convince the owner to transfer the deed to them with the false promise of being able to rent the home back and eventually repurchase it. In reality, the owner loses their home and any equity they had.
  • Appraisal Fraud: This involves a property appraiser deliberately providing a misleading analysis of a property’s true value. Inflated appraisals can trick lenders into approving larger loans and leave buyers with a mortgage that is far more than the property is worth.
  • Equity Skimming: An investor may convince a homeowner to sign over their property, often through a fraudulent sale. The “investor” then rents out the property, collects the payments, and makes no mortgage payments, eventually leading to foreclosure while they pocket the rental income.

Protecting You from a System Designed to Confuse: Jacksonville Mortgage Fraud Attorney

The term “mortgage fraud” often brings to mind borrowers lying on loan applications. In reality, some of the most devastating fraud is perpetrated against borrowers by an ecosystem of predatory lenders, unscrupulous brokers, and even negligent loan servicers.

As your Jacksonville Mortgage Fraud Attorney, we represent homeowners who have been wronged. We are a mortgage lawyer near Jacksonville with deep experience in the federal laws designed to protect you, including the Truth in Lending Act (TILA), the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA), and the protections enforced by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).

If you feel you have been deceived or are being treated unfairly, contact us immediately. We handle cases involving:

1. Predatory Lending & Deceptive Practices

Predatory lending targets vulnerable borrowers—often the elderly, first-time buyers, or those with credit issues—and steers them into loans with abusive terms. This can include:

Bait-and-Switch

Promising you a low, fixed interest rate and then presenting you with a variable-rate loan or a much higher rate at closing.

Loan Flipping

Convincing you to repeatedly refinance your loan, adding no tangible benefit while “packing” new fees and costs into the principal each time.

Hidden Balloon Payments

Obscuring the fact that a massive, all-at-once payment will be due at the end of the loan term, which the lender knows you cannot afford.

Failure to Disclose (TILA Violations)

Failing to provide clear, timely, and accurate disclosures of the loan’s APR, finance charges, and total cost.

2. Loan Servicer Errors & Abuses

Often, the company you make your monthly payments to (the “servicer”) is not the bank that gave you the loan. Servicing companies are notorious for errors that can cost homeowners thousands and even lead to wrongful foreclosure. We fight servicers for:

Misapplication of Payments

Not applying your payments correctly, leading to bogus late fees and negative credit reporting.

Escrow Account Mismanagement

Making dramatic, unexplained increases in your escrow payments for taxes and insurance.

Force-Placed Insurance

Forcing you to buy an extremely expensive homeowner’s insurance policy (that you don’t need) by claiming your own policy has lapsed, then charging you for it.

Illegal Fees

Padding your account with corporate “advances,” inspection fees, and other junk fees not authorized by your mortgage.

RESPA Violations

Ignoring your “Qualified Written Requests” (QWRs) or failing to provide a proper accounting of your loan.

3. Foreclosure Defense & Foreclosure Scams

If you are facing foreclosure, do not wait. You have legal rights. We can provide a robust defense by:

Challenging “Standing”

Forcing the bank to prove they legally own your note—a step they often cannot take due to a broken chain of “assignments.”

Identifying Procedural Errors

Finding mistakes the lender made in the foreclosure process, which can halt the action.

Loss Mitigation

Simultaneously negotiating with the lender for a workable solution, such as a loan modification, forbearance, or short sale, while defending you in court.

We also pursue “foreclosure rescue” scammers—companies that charge you thousands of dollars with a “guarantee” to save your home, only to take your money and disappear.

How Much Does a Mortgage Attorney Cost in Florida?

The cost of a mortgage attorney in Jacksonville, FL depends entirely on the type of legal service you need. There is no single price, as “transactional” work and “litigation” work are billed very differently.

  1. Flat-Fee for Transactional Review

For a homebuyer who simply wants an attorney to review their mortgage and closing documents before they sign, this is typically done on a flat-fee basis. This fee provides you with a comprehensive review, a letter or phone call explaining the terms and any risks, and peace of mind. While this varies, you can expect the cost to be a fraction of what you would pay to fix a problem later. This is the most cost-effective legal service in real estate.

  1. Hourly Billing for Litigation & Disputes

For complex issues like mortgage fraud, foreclosure defense, or loan servicing disputes, the work is billed by the hour. This is because it is impossible to predict how a lender will respond. Will they settle quickly, or will they fight you all the way to a trial?

The Retainer

You will typically pay an “advance fee” or “retainer.” This is your money, held in a special trust account. Your attorney bills their time against this retainer as they work on your case.

Hourly Rates

Your legal agreement will state the attorney’s hourly rate. At Lieberman Chamberlain, we are transparent about our billing and provide detailed invoices so you know exactly what work is being done.

While hiring a Jacksonville Mortgage Fraud Attorney on an hourly basis may seem costly, you must consider the alternative. The cost of losing your home or overpaying tens of thousands of dollars on a fraudulent loan is infinitely higher. The cost of an attorney is an investment in protecting your single largest asset.
