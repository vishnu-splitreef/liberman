Secure Your Investment: Expert Title Search Lawyer in Jacksonville, FL

Purchasing real estate in Florida—whether it’s a family home in Jacksonville, a commercial property, or a parcel of land—is one of the most significant financial commitments you will ever make. The excitement of the transaction can often overshadow the critical legal due diligence required to protect that investment. The most fundamental piece of this protection is a comprehensive title search.

A property’s history can be complex and fraught with hidden issues. A “cloud” on the title, such as an unresolved lien, an ownership dispute, or an improper filing from decades past, can jeopardize your right to the property.

Property owners in Jacksonville often learn about a title problem at the worst possible moment, such as when a buyer’s title company flags an old lien, a lender refuses to refinance, or a title insurer declines coverage. A quiet title action asks a Florida court to review the competing claims and issue a judgment that settles who owns the property. Common situations include property bought at a tax deed sale, inherited homes where heirs were never formally resolved, gaps in the chain of title, and old mortgages or liens that were paid but never released. Working with a Jacksonville quiet title attorney early can help you understand your options and the likely timeline before a sale or closing date is at risk.

What is a Title Search and Why is it Essential in Florida?

A title search is a deep-dive investigation into the public records associated with a specific piece of real estate. Its purpose is to verify the seller’s legal right to transfer ownership and to discover any claims, defects, liens, or encumbrances that could affect your ownership.

Think of it as the property’s “background check.” A Jacksonville attorney for title searches will meticulously examine the “chain of title”—the complete history of ownership—and look for any red flags, including:

Outstanding Mortgages

Mortgages from previous owners that were never properly “satisfied” (paid off) in the public record.

Property Liens

Involuntary claims against the property, such as mechanic’s liens (from unpaid contractors), tax liens (from unpaid property or federal taxes), or judgment liens (from a lawsuit).

Easements

Legal rights for another party to use a portion of your property (e.g., a utility company or a neighbor’s right of access).

Deed Restrictions & Covenants

Rules and restrictions (CC&Rs) from a Homeowners Association (HOA) or developer that limit how you can use the property.

Probate & Heirship Issues

Unresolved ownership interests from a deceased former owner.

Fraud or Forgery

Illegitimate deeds or forged signatures in the property’s history.

Encroachments

A structure (like a fence or shed) from a neighboring property that is illegally crossing onto your land.

Without a professional title search, you could inherit these problems. You might find yourself paying off someone else’s debt or, in a worst-case scenario, defending your ownership in court.

Your Florida Title Search FAQs

As a leading title search lawyer in Jacksonville, FL, we hear the same critical questions from prospective buyers. Here are the clear, straightforward answers you need.

Q.01How much does a title search cost in FL?

This is one of the most common questions, and the answer depends on the complexity of the search. The cost of a title search in Florida is not a single, fixed price. It’s influenced by several factors:

Search Type

A basic “current owner” search is less expensive than a “full” search, which traces the title back 30 years or more as required by Florida’s Marketable Record Title Act (MRTA).

Property History

A new home in a planned subdivision often has a very simple, clear chain of title. An older property in a historic Jacksonville neighborhood or a rural parcel of land that has been divided multiple times will have a much more complex history, increasing the search time and cost.

Search vs. Examination

The cost you are quoted often separates the search (the raw data pull) from the examination (the legal analysis). The search itself might cost between $150 and $400. However, the true value is in the attorney’s examination and a formal Title Opinion Letter. This legal analysis is what interprets the data and identifies potential risks.

When you work with Lieberman Chamberlain, we provide a transparent quote for the complete title search, examination, and closing services as part of your real estate transaction.

Q.02Can I do my own title search in Florida?

Legally, the answer is yes. Florida’s public records, typically held by the County Clerk of the Court’s office (like the Duval County Clerk of Courts), are open to anyone. You can, in theory, go to the office or use their online portals to trace the chain of title yourself.

Practically, however, this is an incredibly risky decision. Here’s why:

Interpreting Legal Documents

Property records are filled with complex legal jargon (“grantor,” “grantee,” “lienholder,” “lis pendens,” “quitclaim deed”). Misinterpreting one document can cause you to miss a critical defect.

Missing Records

You may not know all the places to look. Records can be filed in different offices (e.g., probate court, tax collector).

Abstracting Errors

A professional title abstractor is trained to follow the chain of title and identify gaps or “clouds.” It is a meticulous skill.

No Recourse

If you make a mistake, you have no one to blame but yourself. You cannot purchase “Errors & Omissions” (E&O) insurance for a search you perform yourself. If a title search lawyer in Jacksonville, FL makes a mistake, their E&O insurance protects you.

No Title Insurance

Most importantly, no title insurance underwriter will issue a policy based on a DIY search. Without title insurance, no lender will give you a mortgage.

Hiring a real estate attorney for a title search in Jacksonville, FL is a small price to pay for professional protection and peace of mind.

Q.03How long does it take to do a title search in Florida?

The timeframe for a professional title search can vary. On average, you can expect a title search and examination to take 5 to 10 business days.

This timeline can be affected by:

County Backlogs

The speed of the county recorder’s office.

Property Age & Complexity

As mentioned, older properties with complex histories take longer.

Resolving Defects

The initial search may be quick, but if it uncovers a defect (like an old lien), the process of resolving that defect—called “curative work”—can take weeks or even months.

This is why it’s crucial to begin the title search process as soon as you are under contract for a property.

Why You Need a Real Estate Attorney, Not Just a Title Company

Many title companies can perform a title search and issue a title insurance policy. However, they cannot provide legal advice.

This distinction is critical.

  • A title company’s job is to identify risk and insure against it.
  • A real estate attorney’s job is to identify risk, provide legal counsel on it, and actively work to resolve it.

As your Jacksonville attorney for title searches, Lieberman Chamberlain offers comprehensive legal representation. If we find a cloud on the title, we don’t just “note it as an exception” on your insurance policy. We advise you on the best course of action. This may include:

  • Negotiating with the seller to fix the issue before closing.
  • Contacting previous lienholders to obtain a release or satisfaction of mortgage.
  • Advising you on the risks of proceeding with the purchase.
  • Filing a “Quiet Title” lawsuit—a legal action to get a court order that resolves all ownership disputes and clears the title once and for all.

Only a licensed attorney can represent you in court to quiet a title. This is the highest level of protection you can have.
