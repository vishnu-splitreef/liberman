The Invisible Injury: Types and Severity of Traumatic Brain Injuries

A TBI occurs from a violent blow, jolt, or penetrating injury to the head or body that disrupts normal brain function. Traumatic brain injuries are a subset of head and brain injuries, which can result from various types of trauma. The severity is often classified using the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), but the long-term impact is unique to every victim.

There are two primary types of traumatic brain injury: open-head injuries and closed-head injuries. Open-head injuries happen when an external object penetrates the skull and causes brain damage. Closed-head injuries occur when the trauma damages the brain without penetrating the skull. The skull plays a crucial role in protecting the brain, but both open and closed injuries can have severe consequences.

Our brain injury lawyers in Jacksonville, FL handle cases across the full spectrum of TBI severity, including:

  • Concussions
  • Contusions
  • Diffuse axonal injuries
  • Coup-contrecoup injuries
  • Brain bleed (bleeding within or around the brain that can require emergency intervention)
  • Penetrating injuries

Each injury caused by trauma or internal factors can result in significant damage or harm to the brain. In addition to traumatic injuries, non traumatic brain injuries, such as those caused by strokes or other medical conditions, can also result in serious brain damage.

1. Mild Traumatic Brain Injury (MTBI) / Concussion

While often dismissed as minor, an MTBI or concussion is a real brain injury. A significant percentage of patients develop Post-Concussion Syndrome (PCS), enduring persistent symptoms for months or even years.

Symptoms

Persistent headaches, dizziness, sensitivity to light/noise, memory problems, sleep disturbances, irritability, and difficulty concentrating.

The Legal Challenge

Insurance companies often minimize concussions. We fight to prove that the lingering cognitive and emotional deficits are permanent and warrant significant long-term compensation.

2. Moderate to Severe TBI

These injuries involve a more significant period of unconsciousness, confusion, or coma, often resulting in permanent structural damage to the brain.

Specific Injuries
  • Cerebral Contusions: Bruising of the brain tissue.
  • Hematomas (Epidural/Subdural): Bleeding inside or on the surface of the brain, causing dangerous pressure.
  • Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI): Microscopic shearing and tearing of the brain’s white matter fibers, often leading to severe, permanent impairment.
Life-Altering Consequences

Paralysis, permanent motor/sensory deficits, speech and language problems (aphasia), debilitating seizures, cognitive disabilities, and the need for lifelong supportive care.

Leading Causes of TBI in Jacksonville

Traumatic brain injuries are a leading cause of disability and are extremely common in the U.S., with about 1.5 million Americans sustaining brain injuries each year. Brain injuries most frequently occur due to another party’s negligence, often in high-impact situations common in the Jacksonville area:

Motor Vehicle Accidents (MVA)

Car, truck, and motorcycle collisions, especially those involving rapid acceleration and deceleration (whiplash effect), which can cause Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI) even without direct head impact. Many TBIs result from an accident caused by someone else’s negligence, and accident victims have legal rights to pursue compensation for their injuries.

Falls

The most common cause of TBI overall, particularly in older adults. These include slip-and-fall and trip-and-fall accidents due to unmaintained property or dangerous conditions (Premises Liability). Wet floors are a frequent hazard that can lead to serious head and brain injuries, as well as other injuries.

Pedestrian and Bicycle Accidents

Unprotected individuals hit by vehicles suffer catastrophic head and body trauma.

Sports & Recreational Accidents

Improper supervision, defective equipment, or lack of proper safety protocol. Sports injuries, especially concussions, are prevalent in youth athletics and can lead to long-term health issues.

Workplace/Construction Accidents

Falls from heights, scaffolding collapses, or being struck by falling objects.

Medical Malpractice

Injuries or oxygen deprivation during birth (birth trauma) or surgical procedures.

Most brain injuries are preventable and often result from everyday accidents and risky activities. TBI victims face significant challenges, including long-term disabilities and financial burdens, highlighting the importance of legal and medical support for accident victims.

The Full Price of a TBI: Calculating Damages for a Lifetime

The compensation in a TBI case must account for the injury’s full impact over the victim’s entire expected lifespan. Unlike a simple broken bone, a brain injury creates compounding losses that require specialized expert testimony to calculate. Economic damages include any damage that comes with a measurable dollar value, such as medical bills and lost wages. Non-economic damages are more complicated to calculate and include compensation for pain and suffering, emotional distress, and diminished quality of life. Identifying responsible parties is crucial for recovering full compensation in TBI cases.

At Lieberman Chamberlain, we work with a network of neurologists, life-care planners, neuropsychologists, and vocational experts to ensure every dollar of your loss is documented and pursued. A qualified TBI firm should have an established network of specialists, including neurologists, neuropsychologists, and economic experts to calculate long-term care costs.

There is no “average,” as the value is determined entirely by the individual’s prognosis and documented needs.

Why TBI Cases Demand Specialized Legal Representation

Insurance companies and their defense attorneys are trained to exploit the unique difficulties presented by brain injury cases:

1. The “Invisible Injury” Defense

Unless the injury is severe and instantly visible on an MRI (like a hematoma), the defense will often claim the victim is exaggerating or faking their symptoms (e.g., memory loss, personality change, constant headaches).

  • Our Counter: We utilize advanced diagnostic tools like Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) and detailed neuropsychological testing to objectively document the functional and structural damage to the brain, providing irrefutable evidence.

2. Undiagnosed or Delayed Diagnosis

Symptoms of TBI, especially concussions, can sometimes appear days or weeks after the initial trauma, making it harder to link them directly to the accident.

  • Our Counter: We work backward, linking the initial traumatic event to the delayed onset of symptoms, often through detailed analysis of initial medical records, witness statements, and the patient’s immediate post-accident behavior.

3. Future Costs and Lifetime Care

The defense will always attempt to lowball the cost of future care. A non-specialized attorney may accept a settlement that runs out in a few years, leaving the victim bankrupt.

  • Our Counter: We employ certified Life Care Planners who build a comprehensive, document-supported projection of costs for the victim’s remaining life expectancy, ensuring the final compensation is adequate for all future needs.

Florida Statute of Limitations: The Critical Deadline

Time is a critical factor in a TBI claim. Under Florida law, the Statute of Limitations for most personal injury claims, including those involving TBI, is two (2) years from the date of the injury-causing accident. In certain cases, exceptions to the standard statute of limitations may apply, so it is important to discuss your specific situation with a Jacksonville traumatic brain injury lawyer.

If the lawsuit is not filed within this two-year deadline, you permanently lose your right to seek compensation. Given the need for extensive medical investigation, neuropsychological testing, and the appointment of experts, you must contact a Jacksonville traumatic brain injury lawyer immediately.

If the TBI resulted in the permanent legal incapacitation of the victim, there may be exceptions to the two-year rule, allowing more time to file—but only a specialized attorney can properly invoke these exceptions.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) About TBI Claims in Florida

Q.01Q: What is the difference between a “brain injury” and a “concussion” in a lawsuit?

A: A concussion is formally classified as a mild Traumatic Brain Injury (MTBI). Legally, they are both considered TBI. While a concussion may result in a shorter recovery, a lawsuit involving a Jacksonville brain injury concussion lawyer focuses on proving that the effects—such as Post-Concussion Syndrome (PCS), chronic headaches, and cognitive problems—are persistent and have permanently impacted the victim’s quality of life and earning ability.

Q.02Q: My doctor said my brain scans (MRI/CT) were “normal.” Can I still have a valid TBI case?

A: Yes, absolutely. Many TBIs, particularly concussions and even severe injuries like Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI), are not visible on standard imaging like CT scans or routine MRIs. These injuries involve microscopic damage to brain cells and neural pathways. Our firm counters the “normal scan” defense by using advanced evidence like Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) and comprehensive neuropsychological testing, which are the standard for diagnosing functional TBI deficits.

Q.03Q: How can a lawyer prove the true value of my future TBI care needs?

A: Proving future value requires specialized evidence and witnesses. We retain a Certified Life Care Planner (CLCP). The CLCP works with your treating physicians to create a detailed document itemizing every expense you will incur over your life expectancy: medication, cognitive therapy, specialized equipment, home modifications, and 24/7 attendant care. This plan is presented as evidence to justify the multi-million-dollar compensation needed.

Q.04Q: What if I was partially at fault for the accident that caused my TBI?

A: Florida operates under the Modified Comparative Fault rule. If you are found partially at fault, your compensation will be reduced by your percentage of fault. For example, if you are awarded $5 million but found 20% at fault, you receive $4 million. However, if you are found more than 50% at fault, you are barred from recovering any damages. Our job is to aggressively minimize any attempt by the defense to shift blame to you.

Q.05Q: How long does it take to settle a TBI lawsuit in Jacksonville?

A: TBI cases take longer to resolve than typical personal injury cases. The legal process often waits until the victim reaches Maximum Medical Improvement (MMI), which can take 12 to 24 months, to accurately assess permanent damage. Once MMI is reached and the life care plan is finalized, the claim may enter aggressive negotiation or trial. Expect a moderate to severe TBI case to take anywhere from 18 months to 3 years or more to resolve, though we work to secure interim funding if possible.
